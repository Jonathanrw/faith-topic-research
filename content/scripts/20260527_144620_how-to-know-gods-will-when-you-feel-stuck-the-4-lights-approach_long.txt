Stuck on a decision? Follow the four lights to God’s will. If you’ve been praying, journaling, and still feel paralyzed, you are not broken or behind. You’re human, and God is not playing hide-and-seek with your future. He is a Shepherd who leads. Today, I want to give you a simple, memorable framework you can return to whenever you feel uncertain: the 4 Lights Approach.

Think of a runway at night. A single light is helpful, but when multiple lights line up, you know it’s safe to land. Discerning God’s will often works the same way. No formula can replace God’s friendship, but these four lights bring clarity when it’s foggy: Scripture, the Spirit’s peace, wise counsel, and God’s providence and timing. And there’s a powerful check that runs beneath them all—desire surrendered to God. Then we’ll end with a 48-hour prayer plan you can start today.

The first light is Scripture. Psalm 119:105 says, “Your word is a lamp to my feet and a light to my path.” A lamp doesn’t always show you the entire highway, but it gives you the next few steps. When you’re stuck, start by asking, what does the Bible clearly say about this? God’s will never contradicts God’s Word. If the decision tempts you to cut corners, to be dishonest, to step into impurity, to ignore love, the lamp has already spoken. Even when Scripture doesn’t name your exact scenario, it reveals God’s character. He cares about truth, humility, servanthood, justice, purity, faithfulness. Hold your options up to those qualities. Which path would honor Jesus most? Which choice would you feel okay reading out loud before Him?

The second light is the Spirit’s peace. Colossians 3:15 says, “Let the peace of Christ rule in your hearts.” That word “rule” means to act like an umpire. In other words, peace doesn’t just soothe; it guides. This isn’t the absence of butterflies. It’s the settled, quiet yes that remains when you pause, breathe, and fix your eyes on Christ. If you’re anxious, remember your nervous system might be in fight-or-flight. Overthinking floods the mind with “what-ifs.” Before you assume God is saying no, slow your body so you can hear your Shepherd. Take a few deep breaths. Whisper the name of Jesus. Hand Him the situation. Then pay attention: when you imagine choice A in His presence, does your spirit unclench, or tighten? What about choice B? Peace is not hype. It’s not the rush of relief you get from escaping pain. It’s the steady confidence that you can move with God, even if the outcome is uncertain. If there is confusion or chronic dread, keep waiting and keep praying. If there is unexplainable calm, take note.

The third light is wise counsel. Proverbs 11:14 says, “Where there is no guidance, a people falls, but in an abundance of counselors there is safety.” Ask two or three mature believers who know the Bible and know you. Invite them to disagree with you. Share what you think God is saying, and ask them to test it. Sometimes God’s direction sounds like your mentor asking a gentle question you didn’t know you needed. Counsel doesn’t replace obedience, but it often protects you from blind spots. And if every wise voice in your life is waving a red flag, pause. There is safety in community.

The fourth light is providence and timing. Revelation 3:7 describes Jesus as the One who opens and no one shuts, and shuts and no one opens. Proverbs 16:9 reminds us that we plan our way, but the Lord establishes our steps. Pay attention to doors. Some open with unusual ease; others stay stubbornly shut no matter how hard you push. Providence includes circumstances, timing, resources, interruptions, and even holy delays. If the first three lights are green but the timing keeps resisting, don’t panic. It may not be a no. It may be a not yet. God shepherds us with both invitations and boundaries.

Underneath all four lights is a surrendered heart. Psalm 37:4 says, “Delight yourself in the Lord, and He will give you the desires of your heart.” That doesn’t mean He rubber-stamps everything we want. It means as we delight in Him, He shapes our want-to. This is where faith meets psychology. Our desires can be tangled up with fear of missing out, people-pleasing, or pain avoidance. So bring your motives into the light. Tell God plainly what you want, and then add, I want You more. If a desire grows brighter as you worship, if it survives the fire of surrender, it may be from Him. If it shrinks when you lay it down, let it go.

But what do you do this week, practically? Here’s a simple 48-hour prayer plan to break the paralysis. Over the next two mornings, start with Scripture. Read Psalm 119:105 slowly. Then read Colossians 3:15. Write your decision as a question: Lord, what would honor You most here? List the options you’re considering. Put an open Bible and an open heart on the table. Ask the Spirit to highlight any verse or principle that applies. If anything conflicts with clear teaching, cross it off.

Around midday on the first day, reach out to two trusted believers. Share concisely what you’re choosing between. Ask them to pray and to be honest. Request their thoughts by the following evening. In the afternoon, take a short walk and pray out loud if you can. Imagine choosing option A while you breathe slowly in God’s presence. Notice your body. Then imagine option B with the same slow, centered attention on Jesus. Jot down what you sense—peace, pressure, avoidance, hope. In the evening, surrender your desire again. Name it. Then pray, Father, I trust Your timing. If this should open, open it. If it should close, close it. I will follow.

On the second morning, check the lights again. Has any Scripture come alive? Did sleep bring clarity? Is there a growing, non-anxious peace about one path? Review any counsel that came in. Do their insights confirm or caution? Look at providence. Did an unexpected door open? Did a necessary resource appear or a major barrier arise? If three or more lights line up in the same direction—Scripture consistent, a settled peace, wise counsel inclined, and providence not resisting—take a humble step. If the lights are mixed, keep the step small and reversible. If everything feels red, wait without self-contempt. Waiting on God is not failure; it’s obedience.

And remember this: God’s will is often a path, not a point. He can steer a moving ship. Sometimes He invites you to choose among good options with freedom, trusting that as you seek first His kingdom, He will guide your steps. Other times, He makes the right path unmistakably clear because He knows you need it. Either way, you are not alone in the fog. The Shepherd walks before you.

If you try the 48-hour plan and still feel stuck, go back to what you know to be God’s will today. Love someone. Tell the truth. Do your work with integrity. Keep a clean heart. Give thanks. These are never wasted steps, and God loves to guide a life that is already moving in obedience. He is not grading you on flawless discernment. He is forming you into the image of His Son.

So when the fear of choosing wrong shows up, talk to it gently. Fear, you don’t get to be my shepherd. Jesus does. Then check the four lights. Open your Bible for the lamp of His Word. Let Christ’s peace rule. Invite wise counsel. Watch for providence and timing. Delight in the Lord until your desires rest in Him. Take the next faithful step, and trust Him to correct your course if needed.
