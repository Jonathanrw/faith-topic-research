Is it God, me, or fear? Use these four tests. If you’ve ever stared at a decision, praying hard and second-guessing even harder, you’re not alone. Overthinking can make God’s guidance feel complicated, but your Father is not playing hide and seek with His will. He speaks. He leads. And He gives you simple, solid ways to discern His voice.

Today I want to give you a clear grid you can return to again and again. Think of it as four windows you look through: the Word test, the Way of Christ test, the Wisdom of counsel test, and the Witness of fruit and peace. These are anchored in Scripture so you can stop spiraling and start walking with confidence.

First, the Word test. Second Timothy 3:16 says all Scripture is God-breathed and useful for teaching, correcting, and training in righteousness. God will never lead you to do something that contradicts His Word or His character revealed in it. That means any impression that pushes you toward dishonesty, impurity, resentment, or pride is not from Him. It also means God’s voice often rises from the pages of Scripture as a living word that meets your situation today. If you’re unsure, anchor your thoughts in what is clear, not in what is cloudy. Let the Bible be the guardrail before it’s the afterthought.

Second, the Way of Christ test. Jesus said, My sheep hear My voice in John 10:27. Ask, does this sound like Jesus? The voice of Jesus is truthful without cruelty, firm without fearmongering, holy yet full of grace. When He corrects, He invites you to repent and return, not to wallow in shame. He is the Good Shepherd, not the harsh accuser. If the voice you’re hearing heaps condemnation with no path to hope, that’s not your Shepherd. If it flatters your ego and shrugs off sin, that’s not Him either. The way of Jesus has a recognizable tone: love, truth, humility.

Third, the Wisdom of counsel test. Proverbs 11:14 says there is safety in an abundance of counselors. God often confirms His leading through the mouths of mature believers who know you, love you, and love God’s Word. One wise voice is helpful; two or three can steady you when emotion pulls hard. If your decision can’t withstand daylight with trusted people, pause. Humility invites clarity. This isn’t crowdsourcing God’s will; it’s honoring the body of Christ and refusing the echo chamber of your own anxieties.

Fourth, the Witness of fruit and peace. Galatians 5:22 describes the fruit of the Spirit—love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, self-control. And Colossians 3:15 says let the peace of Christ rule in your hearts, like an umpire. After you’ve tested the word and the way and the wisdom, what remains in you? Not the absence of nerves—that can still be there—but a deep, settled “yes” that aligns with the Spirit’s fruit. Be careful not to confuse the calm of avoidance with the peace of obedience. Avoidance says, I feel relief because I ran away. Obedience says, I feel a steady peace even while my knees shake, because I’m moving with God.

Let’s walk this through a real decision. Imagine you’ve been offered a job in another city. It pays more, promises a faster career path, but will pull you away from your current church and support system. Part of you is excited. Part of you is terrified. And your brain won’t stop spinning.

You start with the Word test. You ask, what does Scripture say about work, provision, calling, and community? You read passages like Proverbs on diligence, Matthew 6 on seeking first the kingdom, Hebrews 10 on not neglecting to meet together. Nothing in the offer is sinful on its face. But Scripture elevates faithfulness and community, and warns against chasing wealth as a master. The Word doesn’t give you a direct yes or no, but it gives you rails: provision is good, but not at the cost of your soul. God cares about your church life, your home life, your integrity.

Now the Way of Christ test. As you sit with the Gospels, you ask, if Jesus were physically here, how would He speak to me about this? What tone would He take? You remember how Jesus tends to invite people to follow Him personally, not just to chase outcomes. You sense His questions: Will this move help you love Me and others more? Will it open space to serve or slowly throttle your soul? Does this choice grow humility and dependence, or feed pressure and performance? You notice that the strongest pressure in you is fear of missing out and the need to prove yourself. That doesn’t sound like your Shepherd. But you also sense His kindness: I’m not trying to trap you. I want you to be whole with Me.

Next, the Wisdom of counsel test. You call your small group leader and an older believer who’s walked with Jesus for decades. You lay out the facts and your wrestle, and you ask them to be honest. One asks about the spiritual health of the city and the churches there. Another asks how the job will affect your time with God and your potential future family rhythms. Both remind you that provision is God’s job and that no door remains open if it will destroy your walk with Him. One says, if you go, go because God is leading, not because you’re scared to stay. The counsel doesn’t hand you a verdict, but it adds ballast to your boat. You feel seen and steadied.

Finally, the Witness of fruit and peace. After praying, fasting a meal, and sleeping on it, you check your heart. When you picture staying, you feel relief but also a small nudge that you might be avoiding growth. When you picture going, you feel nervous, but also a quiet excitement to engage a new church family and trust God in fresh ways. You’re not high on adrenaline. You sense a measurable peace that draws you toward dependency on Christ, not on a paycheck or on people’s approval. That peace becomes your umpire.

Do you see how the grid slows your racing mind without shutting it down? The Word grounds you. The Way of Christ clarifies the tone. The Wisdom of counsel anchors you in community. The Witness of fruit and peace guides your next step. Not a magic formula, but a faithful pattern. And if at any point a test fails—if it clearly contradicts Scripture, if it sounds unlike Jesus, if wise counsel raises red flags, or if there is no fruit but only chaos—then wait. God isn’t late when He’s protecting you. James 1:5 promises that if you lack wisdom, ask God, and He gives generously without finding fault.

Here’s a 24-hour practice plan you can start today. Set aside ten quiet minutes with your Bible open to a Gospel passage and ask God to highlight His heart. Write your decision at the top of a page and briefly note what Scripture says that’s relevant. Then, in prayer, ask, if this were Jesus speaking, what would His tone be? Write that down in a sentence. Send a message to one or two mature believers and invite their honest feedback. After that, put your phone away for the evening, go to bed in trust, and in the morning check your heart for the kind of peace that comes with obedience. If you sense that peace, take one small, concrete step in that direction—a phone call, a calendar block, or a draft email. If you don’t, keep waiting and keep asking. God’s will is not a maze; it’s a relationship.

If you’re afraid of getting it wrong, hear this: your Father is better at leading than you are at missing it. Psalm 119 says His Word is a lamp to your feet, not a floodlight to your year. Lamps are enough for the next step. He delights to guide you step by step, and He knows how to redirect you if needed. Stay near His Word. Stay near His people. Stay sensitive to His peace. Over time, the sound of His voice will become familiar, like the voice of a friend you can recognize in a crowded room.
