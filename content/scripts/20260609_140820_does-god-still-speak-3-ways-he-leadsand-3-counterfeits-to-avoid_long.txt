God speaks. So does anxiety. Learn the difference. If you’ve ever begged God for an answer and felt paralyzed by the noise, this is for you. You’re not broken for struggling to discern His voice. You’re human. And God isn’t playing hide-and-seek with your heart.

The Bible is honest about this tension. Jesus said, “My sheep hear my voice” in John 10:27, and Scripture also tells us that God’s word is a lamp to our feet in Psalm 119:105. So yes, God still speaks. He has spoken finally and clearly in Jesus, and He continues to lead His people by His word, by His Spirit, and through His providence. By the end of this video, you’ll know three primary ways God leads, three counterfeits to avoid, and a simple six-question checklist you can replay whenever you need to discern His voice.

First, God leads us through Scripture. Second Timothy 3 says all Scripture is God-breathed and equips us for every good work. The Bible isn’t a magic eight ball; it’s living and active, shaping how we see God, ourselves, and our decisions. When you’re unsure, start with what God has clearly said. If you’re anxious, Philippians 4 calls you to pray with thanksgiving and receive His peace. If you’re angry, Ephesians 4 calls you to put away bitterness and forgive. When a decision isn’t directly addressed, Scripture still trains your instincts—what it values, you value; what it forbids, you avoid; what it celebrates, you pursue. The more you soak in God’s word, the more His tone becomes familiar. A good practice is to slow down, read a passage out loud, ask what it reveals about God’s character, and then obey the clearest next step. Clarity often comes on the other side of simple obedience.

Second, God leads by the Spirit’s promptings that are always in line with Scripture. Romans 8 says those who are led by the Spirit are God’s children. The Spirit nudges us to confess a hidden sin, to encourage someone specifically, to give generously, to choose gentleness over retaliation. These promptings carry the fragrance of Jesus. They don’t contradict the Bible, they don’t demand you ignore wisdom, and they carry the fruit of the Spirit—love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control. Sometimes a person comes to mind during prayer, and you sense, “Reach out.” You do, and find they needed it. That’s not random. The Spirit loves to build up the body of Christ and glorify Jesus, not draw attention to us. We test these impressions with Scripture, time, and wise counsel.

Third, God leads through providential circumstances. Proverbs says a person plans their way, but the Lord directs their steps. You prepare, and God opens or closes doors. In Acts 16, Paul wanted to go one direction but was prevented and then redirected by a vision. You might apply for a job, do your best, and suddenly a conversation or opportunity aligns with what you’ve been praying about and what Scripture commends. That’s providence—God quietly weaving your steps. Circumstances alone aren’t infallible, but they often confirm what God’s word and Spirit are already highlighting. Pay attention to the patterns, the timing, and the counsel God places in your path.

Now, three counterfeits that often masquerade as God’s voice. The first counterfeit is fear-driven urgency. It sounds like, “Do this now or you’ll ruin everything.” It feels like a ticking clock in your chest. While God can call you to act promptly, His leading carries peace even in urgency. Colossians 3 says to let the peace of Christ rule in your heart. Panic is tyrannical; God’s voice is firm but fatherly. If fear is pushing you, pause. Let the Shepherd restore your soul before you move.

The second counterfeit is ego-flattering words. This is the message that puts you at the center, that promises special status or applause without the way of the cross. God does affirm our identity in Christ, but His voice produces humility, service, and love. James 3 says wisdom from above is pure, peaceable, gentle, and full of mercy. If what you’re hearing excuses pride, belittles others, or seeks a platform more than God’s presence, be careful. The Holy Spirit exalts Jesus, not our ego.

The third counterfeit is anything that contradicts Scripture. No matter how strong the impression, God will never lead you to sin, to deceive, to break covenant, to indulge hatred, or to worship anything besides Him. Paul warned in Galatians 1 that even if an angel preached a different gospel, it should be rejected. God’s voice agrees with God’s word. If a “leading” requires you to twist verses or ignore the plain teaching of the Bible, it’s not from Him.

So how do you discern between God’s leading and these counterfeits in real time? Here’s a simple six-question checklist you can return to again and again. First, does this align with the clear teaching of Scripture, not just a verse pulled out of context? If the Bible speaks plainly, submit to it. Second, does this move me toward Christlike character and the fruit of the Spirit? If it grows love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, and self-control, it likely reflects His heart. Third, what is the dominant atmosphere as I pray and wait—panic, confusion, and compulsion, or a deepening sense of peace and willingness to trust? God’s peace doesn’t mean zero nerves; it means a settled confidence under the nerves. Fourth, am I willing to obey even if no one ever knows and even if it costs me? If my excitement depends on being seen, my ego may be steering. Fifth, have I submitted this to wise, godly counsel who know Scripture and me? Proverbs says there is safety in an abundance of counselors; don’t isolate big decisions. Sixth, can I remove the artificial deadline and keep praying? If the clarity evaporates when the pressure is gone, it was probably pressure, not providence. God’s timing is perfect; Isaiah 30 says in quietness and trust is your strength.

If you’re still unsure after walking through those questions, wait. Waiting on God is not spiritual failure. It is faith in slow motion. Keep doing the last thing He made clear. Keep showing up in His word. Keep asking for wisdom, because James 1 promises He gives it generously without finding fault. Sometimes God speaks by stillness, shaping your desires so that, in time, you want what He wants and you can move with a clean conscience.

Maybe today you’re facing a choice, and your heart is loud with what-ifs. Breathe. God is not offended by your need for clarity. He delights to guide His children. If anxiety has been imitating the voice of God, let this be your reset: His voice carries truth, His Spirit bears fruit, His word stands forever, and His providence opens doors you don’t have to force.
