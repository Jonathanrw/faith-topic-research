If God feels silent and your thoughts won’t slow down, stay with me. It’s easier than you think—try this 5-minute listening practice. Today I’m going to show you how to hear God’s voice more clearly, even if you overthink everything.

So many of us want to do what God wants, but we second-guess every nudge. Is that God, or just me? What if I mess it up? What if I wait forever and nothing happens? If that’s you, breathe. Jesus did not design hearing Him to be a riddle. In John 10:27 He said, “My sheep hear my voice; I know them, and they follow me.” If you belong to Jesus, you have the capacity to hear Him. It’s part of your spiritual DNA. The problem isn’t that God is silent; the problem is usually the static. Anxiety. Fear. Noise. Expectations. Let’s quiet the static and practice a way of listening that’s biblical, simple, and doable every day.

I call it the S.O.A.K. method. Like soaking seeds in water so they soften and open, this is about softening your heart and opening your ears to the Shepherd’s voice. S stands for Scripture. O stands for Observe. A stands for Ask. K stands for Keep—obey one small nudge.

Start with Scripture. God’s voice never contradicts His Word, and His Word tunes our ears to His tone. Then, Observe. Pay attention to what stands out. A phrase. A word. A feeling of conviction or comfort. Next, Ask. Turn what you read into a conversation. Ask a simple question and expect a simple answer. Finally, Keep. Choose one small, concrete act of obedience that you can do today. Not ten things. One.

Before we practice, here are a few wise safeguards that keep us grounded and free from confusion. First, agreement with Scripture. God won’t lead you to sin or to act outside His character. Second, godly counsel. If you sense a big decision, run it by a mature believer who knows the Bible and loves you. Third, look for the fruit. Galatians 5:22–23 says the Spirit produces love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control. When you follow a nudge and the fruit looks like that, you’re likely hearing Him. If it breeds fear, pride, manipulation, or chaos, slow down. God leads with peace, even when He calls you to courage.

Let’s do a live three-minute practice together right now. You can do this anywhere—your car before work, a quiet corner of your room, a lunch break. For the next moments, let the noise step aside.

Scripture. Listen to these words from Jesus in John 10:27: “My sheep hear my voice; I know them, and they follow me.” Hear it again, slowly: “My sheep hear my voice; I know them, and they follow me.” This is not a maybe. It’s a promise.

Observe. What stands out to you? Maybe the word hear. Maybe the phrase I know them. Maybe follow me. Notice what draws your attention without forcing it. What is this verse saying about God? He knows you personally. What is it saying about you? You are able to hear and you are invited to follow.

Ask. Now, gently ask Jesus a simple question: Lord, what are You saying to me today from this verse? Or, Lord, where are You inviting me to follow You right now? Don’t strain. You don’t have to manufacture anything. Let your mind settle. Often, the first simple, peaceful thought that aligns with Scripture is the nudge. It may be as small as pause before you speak, send encouragement to that person, bring your worry to Me before you reach for your phone, apologize to your spouse, open your Bible before you open your inbox. If you sense nothing specific yet, that’s okay. Sometimes the word is simply I know you. Stay with Me. Keep showing up. That is still God speaking through His Word.

Keep. Choose one small step you can actually do today in response. If you sensed someone’s name, send a text that says, You were on my heart. If you sensed pause, take thirty seconds before your next task to breathe and pray. If you sensed repent, confess to God and make it right with the person. Keep it small. Small obedience keeps the channel clear and builds trust.

That’s it. Scripture. Observe. Ask. Keep. Five minutes, once a day. Do this for a week and notice how the static begins to thin. You’ll start seeing patterns. God repeats Himself with kindness. He doesn’t play hide-and-seek; He trains your ear through practice.

Let me speak to the overthinker in you. You may worry, What if I mistake my thoughts for God’s voice? Remember those safeguards. Does it match the Bible? Would a mature Christian affirm it? Does it bear the Spirit’s fruit in your life? And here’s something that sets you free: God is a better Shepherd than you are a sheep. If you mishear, He knows how to redirect. Psalm 23 says He leads me in paths of righteousness for His name’s sake. His leadership doesn’t depend on your perfection; it flows from His name. You are not walking a tightrope; you are walking with a Guide.

You may say, I tried listening before and felt nothing. That doesn’t mean you’re broken. Relationships deepen through consistency. Think of planting a seed. You don’t see roots on day one, but they are forming. The S.O.A.K. method helps you show up with faithfulness. Some days will feel ordinary; then one day, the ordinary becomes holy because you realize He’s been speaking through it all along.

A few practical tips to help you keep going. Keep a simple listening journal. Write the date, the Scripture, one observation, the question you asked, and the one small step you kept. Review it weekly. You’ll be amazed at the threads God weaves. Keep your questions simple and present-tense. Instead of What is Your entire will for my life, try What is the next right step today. Big direction usually emerges from many small obediences. And keep your heart soft. Confess known sin quickly. Gratitude and repentance clear the static like nothing else.

One more encouragement. God’s voice often sounds like Scripture coming alive at the right moment, like peace where panic used to sit, like a burden to love someone you’d otherwise overlook, like a gentle check before a hurried decision. He doesn’t scream to compete with your chaos. He whispers to lead you out of it. As you practice, you’ll learn the texture of His voice—pure, peaceable, steady, honest, hopeful. James says the wisdom from above is first pure, then peaceable, gentle, open to reason, full of mercy and good fruits. That’s the tone of your Shepherd.

If today stirred something in you, don’t wait. Take five minutes after this video and S.O.A.K. with John 10:27. Read it. Observe one thing. Ask one question. Keep one small step. Tomorrow, do it again. Let the habit, not the hype, shape you. Over time, confusion gives way to clarity, and anxiety gives way to trust, because you’ll discover what Jesus promised was true all along: My sheep hear My voice.
