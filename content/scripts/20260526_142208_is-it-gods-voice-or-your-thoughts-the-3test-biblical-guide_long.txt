God or just your thoughts? Run this 3‑test check in 30 seconds. If you’ve ever stared at the ceiling at 2 a.m. wondering, “Is this God leading me, or am I just overthinking?” you’re not alone. When we confuse God’s voice with inner noise, anxiety spikes and decisions feel heavy. Today, I want to give you a simple, biblical way to cut through the noise—three tests you can use anytime, anywhere, so you can move from second‑guessing to steady obedience.

Let’s start with a promise. In John 10:27 Jesus says, “My sheep hear my voice, and I know them, and they follow me.” If you belong to Jesus, you can learn to recognize His voice. It’s not about being perfect. It’s about becoming familiar with how the Shepherd speaks. Think of this as a filter for your soul. Three tests. One minute. A lighter mental load.

Test one is Scripture alignment. God’s voice will never contradict God’s Word. Second Timothy 3:16 says, “All Scripture is breathed out by God and profitable for teaching, for reproof, for correction, and for training in righteousness.” That means impressions, ideas, gut feelings, even dreams have to pass the Bible test. Ask, what verse supports this? Does this reflect God’s character I see in Jesus?

Here’s a quick role‑play. Your thought says, “Don’t apologize—you’ll look weak.” God’s Word says, “Clothe yourselves with compassion, kindness, humility” and “If possible, so far as it depends on you, live peaceably with all.” Which voice aligns? Humility and peace. Or your thought says, “God is done with you after that mistake.” Scripture answers, “There is now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus.” Condemnation paralyzes and isolates. Conviction from the Spirit is specific, leads to repentance, and ushers you back under grace. The Shepherd corrects, but He never shames His sheep out of the fold. If the impression fails the Scripture test, you can release it.

Test two is Christlike fruit and peace. James 3:17 describes wisdom from above as pure, peaceable, gentle, open to reason, full of mercy and good fruits, impartial and sincere. And Galatians 5 says the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self‑control. When God speaks, He produces His nature in us. This doesn’t mean the path He calls you to will feel easy, but His direction carries a deep, steady peace that doesn’t demand you know every outcome. The enemy whispers panic, pressure, and pride. The Spirit cultivates humility, mercy, and a peace that guards your heart even in uncertainty.

Role‑play it. Thought one says, “Take the job because you’re behind everyone else. Hurry or you’ll miss your only chance.” Notice the flavor: fear of scarcity and pressure. Thought two says, “Take the job. It stretches you, but I will be with you. Prepare wisely, ask for counsel, and step in faith.” The second carries sober joy, patience, and dependence on God. Or, another example: “Post that angry response now—they deserve it” versus “Pause, pray, and respond with truth and gentleness.” One burns bridges. The other builds them. Pay attention to the fruit the thought invites. God’s voice leads you to look like Jesus, not to win an argument or feed your ego.

Test three is wise counsel and providence. Proverbs 11:14 says, “Where there is no guidance, a people falls, but in an abundance of counselors there is safety.” God often confirms His leading through mature believers and through providential circumstances like opened or closed doors. This isn’t superstition. It’s humility. You ask trusted, godly people, “Does this sound like God’s heart? Am I missing anything?” And you watch how God arranges timing, resources, and opportunities. A closed door doesn’t always mean “no” forever, but it might mean “not yet” or “try a different path.” The Shepherd leads both by His word and His rod and staff—comfort sometimes looks like redirection.

Another quick role‑play. You think, “Move to a new city tomorrow. No plan needed. If you really have faith, just leap.” Counsel replies, “Faith isn’t against wisdom. Pray, map a budget, line up work, find a church, then take the step.” Doors begin to open: an interview, a place to stay, a mentor in that city. That convergence is often God’s kindness. Or you think, “Date this person. They don’t share your faith, but you can fix that later.” Counsel and Scripture together say, “Be equally yoked.” If godly people who know your story consistently raise red flags, pay attention. God loves you enough to slow you down.

Now put it together as a 30‑second check. The next time a decision hits your heart, breathe and ask these three questions. Does this align with Scripture and the character of Jesus? Does this produce Christlike fruit and a steady peace rather than panic or pride? Is this confirmed by wise counsel and providence rather than secrecy and isolation? If you get three yeses, walk forward. If one is a no, pause and pray. If two or three are no, release it. Peace grows when your filters are clear.

Let me speak to the person who feels stuck because God seems silent. Silence doesn’t always mean absence. Sometimes God has already spoken in Scripture, and He’s inviting you to act on what you know. Sometimes He is training your ear to recognize the Shepherd in the quiet, so you won’t be tossed by every emotion. And sometimes He is building patience, because timing matters as much as direction. He cares more about walking with you than rushing you to the finish line. Remember, the Good Shepherd leaves the ninety‑nine to find the one. He knows how to get your attention. You don’t have to force it.

Here’s a simple seven‑day practice to grow your discernment. Each day, take five quiet minutes. Read a short passage like John 10, Psalm 23, James 3, or Galatians 5. Ask, “Lord, what are You highlighting for me today?” Write one sentence you sense. Run it through the three tests. Then do one small act of obedience that looks like Jesus—send an encouraging text, confess a small prideful moment, choose patience in traffic, pray for a coworker in secret. By day seven, look back at what God affirmed and how your peace shifted. Discernment grows with repetition, not perfection.

I’ve created a simple one‑page 3‑Test Guide you can print or screenshot so you don’t have to carry this in your head. Keep it in your journal or on your fridge. The more you use it, the lighter your mental load becomes. Over time, you’ll start to notice, Oh, that anxious rush isn’t Him. Oh, that gentle nudge toward humility is. You’ll develop holy instincts rooted in Scripture, shaped by the Spirit, and confirmed in community.

And if you’re worried you’ve already missed God, hear this. The Shepherd is better at leading than you are at following. He can redeem detours. He can course‑correct with grace. Romans 8 tells us there is no condemnation in Christ. When you feel shame, bring it into the light. When you feel fear, bring it to the Shepherd. When you feel stuck, take the next obedient step you know, however small. He delights in directing hearts that are willing.

So today, before you spiral, pause and run the three tests. Does it align with Scripture? Does it produce Christlike fruit and peace? Is it confirmed by wise counsel and God’s providence? Let that be your compass. Keep your Bible open, your heart soft, your pace unhurried, and your ears tuned to the Shepherd’s voice. He is not playing hide and seek with you. He’s guiding, even now.
