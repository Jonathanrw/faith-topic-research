Hearing God or just your thoughts? Use these four filters to know the difference. If you’ve ever prayed, felt a nudge, and then spiraled into overthinking, take a breath. Today I’ll show you a simple, biblical way to test impressions so you can move from anxiety to confidence and peace.

Here’s the promise: by the end of this video you’ll have a clear, repeatable method—four filters you can run any thought or decision through. Think of it as W-F-C-P: Word, Fruit, Counsel, Peace and Providence. Not a formula to control God, but a framework to cooperate with Him.

First, the Scripture alignment filter. Second Timothy 3:16 says all Scripture is God-breathed and useful for teaching, rebuking, correcting, and training in righteousness. God’s voice will never contradict God’s Word. He doesn’t tell you to lie, to get revenge, to compromise your integrity, or to abandon love. If the impression pushes you toward sin, manipulation, or pride, it’s not Him. If the impression calls you to obey what He’s already said—honesty, forgiveness, generosity, purity, humility—you’re on solid ground. Remember, sometimes He highlights a verse to your situation, not to add something new, but to apply what He has already revealed. When you’re unsure, anchor in the clear parts of Scripture while you wait for clarity in the cloudy parts of life.

Second, the Christlike fruit test. Galatians 5:22–23 says the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control. James 3:17 adds that wisdom from above is pure, peaceable, gentle, open to reason, full of mercy and good fruit, impartial and sincere. Ask, what kind of fruit would following this voice produce in me and through me? God’s voice may convict you sharply, but it won’t condemn you hopelessly. Conviction is specific and leads to repentance and freedom; condemnation is vague and leads to shame and paralysis. His wisdom is open to reason, not defensive. It is pure, not manipulative. It is peaceable, not chaos-seeking. If obeying the impression would make you more like Jesus in attitude and action, that’s a strong sign you’re hearing Him.

Third, the wise counsel and community filter. Proverbs 11:14 says in an abundance of counselors there is safety. God often confirms privately what He also affirms through the body of Christ. Seek counsel from people who know Scripture, know your character, and aren’t afraid to tell you the truth. Ask them to poke holes in your idea, not just to cheerlead it. If every person you trust raises the same red flag, slow down. If mature believers from different parts of your life independently say, “This aligns with God’s Word and who He’s shaping you to be,” pay attention. You don’t need unanimous votes to obey God, but humility welcomes testing and correction.

Fourth, the peace and providence filter. Colossians 3:15 says let the peace of Christ rule in your hearts. The word “rule” is like an umpire. As you submit to God, He gives a steadiness that sits deeper than nerves. At the same time, watch providence. Are doors opening in a way you couldn’t force? Are doors closing despite your best efforts? Not every open door is from God and not every closed door is from the enemy, but when inner peace and outward circumstances begin to harmonize under the first three filters, you can walk forward with confidence. Pray, “Lord, open what no one can shut and shut what no one can open,” and then accept His answer in trust.

Let’s practice this with a real-life example. Imagine you’ve been offered a new job. It pays more, but requires some dishonest reporting and will keep you away from your family most evenings. Your current role is stable but not exciting.

Run it through the Word. Scripture calls you to honesty and to do your work as unto the Lord. It calls you to love your family and manage your household well. A role that normalizes deception already conflicts with God’s character revealed in His Word.

Now the fruit. Picture yourself six months in. Would you be growing in love, joy, and peace? Or would you feel fragmented, anxious, cutting corners, rationalizing behavior you once resisted? Wisdom from above is pure and peaceable. If the thought of accepting the offer stirs turmoil because of compromise, that’s data.

Counsel next. You bring this to a mentor, a small group leader, and your spouse. Without knowing what the others said, they all ask you the same two questions: Will this make you more like Christ, and can you keep your priorities in biblical order? Their consensus isn’t about fear of change; it’s about faithfulness.

Finally, peace and providence. You pray and sense a steady no, not from panic but from a settled conviction. Then unexpectedly, your current supervisor invites you into a new project that stretches your gifts without compromising your values. A closed door here, an open door there, and a deep, calm yes or no rising within as you let the peace of Christ rule. The four filters together point the way.

Now a 60-second journal exercise you can use any time. Set a timer for one minute. At the top of a page write the decision or impression in one sentence. Then write four quick lines: Word says… Fruit looks like… Counsel thinks… Peace and Providence… Under “Word says,” jot any Scripture that clearly applies. Under “Fruit looks like,” write the kind of person this choice would shape you into and the attitudes you sense. Under “Counsel thinks,” list two people you will ask or what they’ve already said. Under “Peace and Providence,” note whether you feel a humble steadiness before God and any doors that seem to be opening or closing. End with one sentence: Obeying this would make me more like Jesus because… When the timer ends, bring that page to prayer. If you do this for a week with the same issue, you’ll often see a pattern emerge.

A few gentle reminders as you use the filters. Sometimes God’s voice leads through hard obedience. You may not feel instant comfort, but you can still have a deeper peace. Sometimes your emotions will be loud. That’s normal. Don’t rush silence; just refuse to let anxiety be your counselor. And remember, the Shepherd is better at leading than we are at following. Jesus said, “My sheep hear my voice.” If you belong to Him, you can grow in recognizing Him, not by striving, but by abiding.

Here’s a daily ten-second prayer you can make your rhythm: Father, let Your Word correct me. Let Your Spirit grow Your fruit in me. Surround me with wise counsel. Guide me by Your peace and providence. Speak, Lord. Your servant is listening. In Jesus’ name, amen.
