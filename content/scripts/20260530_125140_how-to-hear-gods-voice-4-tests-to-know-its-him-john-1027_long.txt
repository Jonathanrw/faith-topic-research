Is that God, or just me? You know the feeling. A thought lands in your mind at a red light, a whisper while you’re washing dishes, a nudge to call someone, give something, change direction. Part of you feels stirred. Another part worries you’re making it up. If you’ve ever asked, “How do I know it’s really God?” you’re not alone. And the good news is, Jesus said, “My sheep hear my voice, and I know them, and they follow me” from John 10:27. Hearing Him is not a prize for the elite. It’s the birthright of every believer.

Let’s make this simple and biblical. I want to offer four tests to help you discern God’s voice, a daily listening rhythm you can practice, two common mistakes to avoid, and a three-day hear-and-do plan to build confidence. By the end, you’ll have a way to move from confusion to clarity, from fear to faithful steps.

First, the Scripture alignment test. God’s voice never contradicts God’s Word. Second Timothy 3:16 says all Scripture is God-breathed and useful for teaching, correcting, and training. So if a voice tells you to lie, to feed your pride, to hold a grudge, or to step outside God’s design, that’s not Him. On the flip side, if the nudge moves you to forgive, to repent, to love an enemy, to pursue purity, to seek justice with humility, you are hearing God’s tone. Sometimes the clearest “voice” you’ll ever hear is a verse that rises to the surface and won’t let you go. God’s Spirit loves to highlight what He already said. Before you chase a sign, open your Bible. Align the whisper with the Word.

Second, the Christlike character and fruit test. Galatians 5:22–23 describes the fruit of the Spirit: love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control. God’s voice will lead you into this atmosphere, even when it challenges you. Conviction from the Spirit is firm but gentle; it points to Jesus and offers a path forward. Condemnation, on the other hand, is harsh, hopeless, and vague. One pushes you toward the Cross, the other pushes you away from it. Ask yourself, if I follow this nudge, will it grow love? Will it cultivate patience? Will it produce self-control? Will it look more like Jesus in both aim and attitude? If the fruit is rotten, the voice isn’t the Shepherd.

Third, the wise counsel and unity test. Proverbs 11:14 tells us there is safety in a multitude of counselors. God often confirms His leading through trusted, mature believers who know your story and love Scripture. If you sense a big decision, share it with a pastor, a mentor, a praying friend. Ask them to weigh it with you. Often, the Spirit brings unity. It doesn’t mean everyone will agree instantly, but a steady pattern of godly voices pointing in the same direction is a powerful confirmation. Be humble enough to listen. God’s voice doesn’t fear the light.

Fourth, the peace with conviction test. Colossians 3:15 says, “Let the peace of Christ rule in your hearts.” First Corinthians 14:33 tells us God is not a God of confusion but of peace. This doesn’t mean every step of obedience feels easy. Sometimes your knees will shake. But underneath the nerves, there’s a settled sense that this is right, a peace that doesn’t vanish when you pray, a clean conviction that’s anchored in Christ. Chaos, panic, and relentless pressure to decide immediately rarely come from God. He can speak urgently, but He is never frenzied. Look for peace and clarity, not frantic fear.

Now, how do we become the kind of people who can notice these tests in real time? Build a simple listening rhythm. Think of it as three steps you can practice daily.

First, slow down. Take a deep breath in, then out. For the next sixty seconds, be still. No background noise. No scrolling. Simply whisper, “Speak, Lord, your servant is listening.” Let your shoulders drop. Offer Him your attention. Often, the only reason we can’t hear is because we never stop talking.

Second, open the Bible. Start with a short passage. Read it slowly, maybe twice. Ask, “What does this say about God? What is it saying to me today?” Expect the Holy Spirit to illuminate something—maybe a word, a phrase, a gentle correction, or a clear encouragement. Remember, Jesus is the Word made flesh. If you want to hear His voice, spend time where He has already spoken.

Third, write and weigh. Jot down the impressions, Scriptures, or names that come to mind. Then weigh them with the four tests. Does it align with Scripture? Will it grow Christlike fruit? Is there wise counsel that can confirm it? Do I sense peace with conviction, not chaos? Writing helps you see patterns, and weighing helps you avoid being tossed by every thought.

Let’s clear up two common mistakes that pull us off course. The first mistake is seeking signs without Scripture. We can get so hungry for a miracle or a headline from heaven that we ignore the Book God already wrote. If you rely only on coincidences, license plates, and random feelings, you’ll ride an emotional roller coaster. Signs can confirm, but they should not replace Scripture. Let the Bible be home base, and let any sign play second chair.

The second mistake is ignoring the last clear nudge. Many of us say, “God, speak,” while leaving yesterday’s assignment unfinished. Why would God map out step ten when we’ve resisted step one? If He already prompted you to apologize, to forgive, to simplify, to turn from a sin, or to serve in a small way, start there. Obedience tunes your ear. Disobedience clogs it. Return to the last thing you know He asked of you and do it. Often, clarity comes in motion.

Maybe you’re thinking, I’ve tried and I still feel nothing. Be encouraged. Hearing God is less like catching a lightning strike and more like learning the sound of a friend’s footsteps. It grows with time, trust, and repeated conversations. Don’t despise the small. Don’t rush the process. Your Shepherd is better at speaking than you are at hearing, and He knows how to get your attention.

To help you begin, here’s a simple three-day hear-and-do plan. Day one, find a quiet spot for five minutes. Breathe, be silent for sixty seconds, and pray, “Jesus, I’m listening.” Read John 10:27 slowly. Write one sentence about what it means that He knows you. Ask if there’s one person you’re meant to encourage today, then do it. Day two, repeat the rhythm. Read Colossians 3:15 and Galatians 5:22–23. Ask, “What would peace and fruit look like in one decision I’m facing?” Write it down and share it with a wise friend for counsel. Day three, read 2 Timothy 3:16 and Proverbs 11:14. Ask God to highlight one small step of obedience you’ve been delaying. Weigh it with the four tests. Then act on it, even if it’s awkward. In each step, notice how peace grows as you walk with Him.

If confusion rises again, come back to the tests. Does it match Scripture? Does it look like Jesus? Do wise believers see it too? Is there peace with conviction? These are not hoops to jump through. They are guardrails to keep you close to the Shepherd on a winding road.

You don’t have to manufacture God’s voice. You are His. He knows how to lead you. Keep showing up. Keep opening His Word. Keep doing the next right, small thing. Over time, you’ll find that the question, “Is that God, or just me?” becomes, “That’s my Shepherd. I know His voice.”

If this encouraged you, subscribe for more faith and encouragement, share it with someone who needs peace today, and comment, “Speak, Lord, I’m listening.”
