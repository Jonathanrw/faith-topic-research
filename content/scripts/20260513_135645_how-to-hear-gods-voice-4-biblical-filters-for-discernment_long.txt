Is that God or just me? When the stakes are high and the noise is loud, that question can keep you up at night. Today, let’s quiet the confusion with four simple biblical filters that help you recognize the Shepherd’s voice.

If you’ve ever prayed, “Lord, I want to do your will, but I don’t want to get this wrong,” you’re not alone. We live with competing voices—fear that shouts, desire that whispers, the inner critic that accuses, and the Spirit who speaks with a steady, loving firmness. God isn’t playing hide and seek with your heart. He delights to lead you. So when you sense a nudge, a thought, or a direction, run it through these four tests, and watch the fog lift.

First, the Scripture filter: Does it align with God’s Word? Hebrews 4:12 says the word of God is living and active, and it judges the thoughts and attitudes of the heart. That means the Bible isn’t just a book; it’s a light that reveals what’s true about God and about us. God will never contradict what He’s already spoken. If the idea invites you into what Scripture calls sin, or asks you to cut corners on integrity, purity, or love, that’s not God. But this filter is more than avoiding wrong; it’s also recognizing what God consistently values—humility, mercy, faithfulness, justice, reconciliation, generosity, and trust. When a thought echoes those patterns, you’re hearing something in harmony with His voice.

Second, the fruit filter: Will this produce Christlike character? Galatians 5:22–23 describes the fruit of the Spirit—love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control. God’s guidance doesn’t just aim at right outcomes; it forms a Christlike heart along the way. If following this nudge is likely to deepen love, increase patience, or grow gentleness, that’s a good sign. If it feeds pride, fuels bitterness, or requires deception, it’s a red flag. Fruit takes time, but the direction of the decision should lean toward becoming more like Jesus, not less.

Third, the peace filter: Is there the Spirit’s ruling peace, not panicked pressure? Colossians 3:15 says, let the peace of Christ rule in your hearts. That word “rule” is like an umpire. God’s peace doesn’t mean you’ll feel zero nerves; courage often comes with butterflies. But beneath the butterflies, there’s a settled yes, a quiet assurance that God is with you. Pressure screams, “Hurry, or you’ll lose everything.” Peace says, “Wait or go, but you’re held.” Condemnation shoves; conviction invites. When urgency comes from fear of missing out, it mimics guidance without the Shepherd. When direction comes with a steady, anchored sense of God’s nearness, that’s the peace that umpires your heart.

Fourth, the counsel filter: What do wise, godly voices say? Proverbs 11:14 tells us there is safety in a multitude of counselors. Guidance often becomes clearer in the presence of community. Don’t only seek agreement; seek wisdom. Share what you’re sensing with mature believers who know Scripture and know you. Ask them to push back, to pray, to ask the questions you might be avoiding. God loves to confirm His leading through the body of Christ.

Let’s run a real scenario through these filters. Imagine you get a late-night message about a job in another city. It promises big money and a fast track, but they want you to resign this week and keep it quiet from your current employer. Your heart races. It feels like a door opened, but also like a timer started.

Filter one, Scripture. Taking a new job isn’t wrong, but secrecy and lack of integrity raise alarms. Scripture calls us to honesty, to keeping our word, to doing what’s right even when it costs us. That’s a snag. Filter two, fruit. Would rushing this decision cultivate love, patience, and self-control, or would it feed anxiety, greed, and impatience? It leans toward the latter. Filter three, peace. Do you sense conviction with clarity, or fear with frenzy? The knot in your stomach and the fear of missing out suggest pressure, not the ruling peace of Christ. Filter four, counsel. You reach out to two trusted mentors. Both caution you to slow down, vet the company, and not violate your current commitments. Four filters, one answer: pause. Waiting here isn’t disobedience; it’s alignment.

Now let’s consider a different nudge. You sense God prompting you to reach out to a family member you’ve been estranged from and to own your part in the conflict. It’s not glamorous. It’s vulnerable. But it’s persistent.

Scripture? Jesus calls us to reconcile, to forgive, to make peace as far as it depends on us. That aligns. Fruit? Humility, gentleness, patience, and love will grow as you obey. Peace? You feel nervous, but underneath is a tender weight, a quiet yes. Counsel? A mature friend prays with you and says, “I’ve sensed the same for you. I’m with you.” Four filters, one answer: move forward, gently and in faith.

These filters protect you from two equal and opposite errors—rushing ahead in impulse and freezing in overthinking. If you’re prone to analysis paralysis, let these be your rails. Ask, does this align with Scripture? Does it move me toward Christlike fruit? Is there a steady peace, not manipulative pressure? What do wise counselors see? If it clears, act. If it snags, wait. Either way, you are not failing God by being faithful. He is a better Shepherd than you are a sheep, and He knows how to nudge, slow, and redirect.

A word about emotion: strong feelings don’t disqualify guidance, but they don’t define it either. Desire can be a clue; it can also be a counterfeit. God often shapes our desires to match His will, but He also teaches us to test desires in the light of Scripture, fruit, peace, and counsel. And a word about silence: if you don’t sense a fresh word, return to the last clear one. Be faithful there. Keep your Bible open, your heart soft, and your pace unhurried. Ask for wisdom, then trust that He gives it generously.

Sometimes you will step out and still wonder if you heard right. That’s okay. Guidance is not a tightrope; it’s a life with God. If you misstep, He doesn’t mock you; He mentors you. Grace covers the gaps while truth keeps you on course. Let these four filters become muscle memory: Scripture, fruit, peace, counsel. Pray them slowly. Journal your impressions. Invite trusted voices in. And then take the next small step.

The good news is this: Jesus didn’t say, “My scholars hear my voice,” or “My perfect ones hear my voice.” He said, “My sheep hear my voice, I know them, and they follow me.” That’s John 10:27. If you belong to Him, you can grow in recognizing Him. He is patient. He is clear. He is near.
