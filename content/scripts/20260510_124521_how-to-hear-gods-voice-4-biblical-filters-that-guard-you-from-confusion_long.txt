Is it God, my anxiety, or just noise? If you’ve been praying but second-guessing everything, run it through these four filters and watch the confusion begin to lift. This isn’t a formula to control God; it’s a biblical way to quiet the static and recognize the Shepherd’s voice.

First, I want you to breathe. God isn’t playing hide-and-seek with you. He loves to speak through His Word, by His Spirit, and within His people. Often, the problem isn’t that God is silent—it’s that our fears are loud. So here are four filters that bring clarity when you’re overthinking, anxious, or unsure which path to take.

Filter one is Scripture alignment. All Scripture is God-breathed and useful for teaching, rebuking, correcting, and training in righteousness, which means God’s voice will never contradict God’s Word. That’s 2 Timothy 3:16. When a thought or impression comes, hold it up to the whole counsel of Scripture. Does it line up with the character of God revealed from Genesis to Revelation? Does it call you toward love, holiness, humility, integrity, and obedience? Or does it make excuses for sin, inflate your ego, or isolate you from accountability? For example, if you think God is telling you to cut corners at work because “the ends justify the means,” that fails the Word test. If you feel urged to confess, make amends, forgive, or act with courage and purity, that’s the kind of alignment Scripture consistently commends. Don’t just cherry-pick a verse that says what you want; read in context. God’s voice won’t pressure you into violating what He’s already clearly said.

Filter two is Christlike fruit. Jesus said, “By their fruit you will recognize them,” in Matthew 7:16. His voice produces His character over time. Think of love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control. When you sense direction from God, ask what kind of fruit will grow if you follow it. If the impulse pushes secrecy, pride, bitterness, or a frantic hurry that tramples people, slow down. That’s likely your fear, not your Father. The Lord may call you into hard things, but His voice carries a steadying clarity and a trajectory toward Christlikeness. It won’t lead you to lash out, manipulate, or escape responsibility. Fruit takes time to grow, so this filter is both a test and a timeline. Where the Spirit is leading, He also supplies grace to become more like Jesus in the process.

Filter three is wise counsel. Proverbs 11:14 says, “In an abundance of counselors there is safety.” God often confirms His guidance through mature believers who know Scripture and know you. When you’re wrestling with a decision, share it with someone who will open a Bible with you, ask hard questions, and speak truth without flattery. Don’t just gather opinions from friends who agree with you; seek those who fear the Lord more than they fear awkward conversations. Wise counsel is not outsourcing obedience—it’s inviting the body of Christ to help you see your blind spots. If three godly voices independently caution you to slow down, that’s a mercy. If they prayerfully affirm what you sense and show you Scripture you hadn’t considered, that’s also a mercy. Let counsel bring humility and safety to your discernment.

Filter four is peace with conviction. Colossians 3:15 says, “Let the peace of Christ rule in your hearts.” That word “rule” means to act like an umpire. The Spirit’s peace doesn’t always feel comfortable, but it is settled. It carries a holy weight—a calm “yes” paired with the courage to obey. Anxiety relief and Spirit peace are not the same thing. Anxiety relief often says, “Do whatever stops the discomfort right now.” Spirit peace says, “Take the next faithful step, even if your knees shake.” When you wait before God and sense a quiet, persistent conviction that aligns with Scripture and is affirmed by wise counsel, let that peace call the shot. If confusion, chaos, or double-mindedness grows, return to stillness. God isn’t cruel. He knows how to make a path clear in His time.

Let’s put this into practice with a simple three-minute listening prayer you can use any day. First minute: settle and surrender. Say, “Father, I come to listen, not perform. Speak, for Your servant is listening.” Breathe slowly, and read a short Scripture out loud—maybe Psalm 23:1 or John 10:27. Let the Word tune your heart like a guitar before a song. Release your timeline and your need to control the outcome. If anxious thoughts rush in, don’t fight them; hand them to Jesus and say, “You lead.”

Second minute: ask and attend. Ask one clear question, not ten. Something like, “Lord, how do You want me to respond to this opportunity?” or “What is my next faithful step in this relationship?” Then listen. You’re not trying to hear an audible voice. You’re attending to the gentle nudges the Spirit often brings—Scriptures recalled, a sense of warning, a picture of a next step, a person to call, a sin to confess, a boundary to draw. Whatever surfaces, immediately run it beneath filter one and two. Does it align with Scripture? Would it bear Christlike fruit?

Third minute: respond and record. Thank God for anything that aligns with His Word. Ask for confirmation through counsel and peace. Commit to take one small step of obedience you can do today. Then write it down. Don’t demand a full blueprint. Faith often looks like headlights on a dark road—enough for the next few feet.

To make this rewatchable and practical, keep a simple journal using the same format each time. Start with the date and the question you asked. Note the Scriptures you read. Jot the impressions you sensed that align with Scripture and the fruit you believe this could grow in you over time. Write the names of the wise counselors you will check in with this week and any confirmations they offer. Finish with one next faithful step and a short prayer of surrender like, “Lord, I will obey in the light I have; increase my light and my love.” Over weeks and months, you’ll build a track record with God’s faithfulness you can revisit on the days when doubt and dryness whisper loud.

Let me paint a quick example. You’re offered a new job in another city. Your fear says, “Take it now or you’ll miss out.” In prayer, you read James and Proverbs and feel nudged toward seeking counsel and guarding integrity. You sense, “Not yet—prepare.” That aligns with Scripture’s wisdom about planning diligently and avoiding hasty decisions. The fruit would be patience, stewardship, and unity with your family. You talk to two mature believers and your pastor, and each independently encourages more due diligence and a family fast before deciding. As you wait, a quiet conviction settles that if the door is from the Lord, it won’t require manipulation to keep it open. Days later, the company updates the offer with clearer terms and time to transition. Scripture alignment, Christlike fruit, wise counsel, and peace with conviction—all four filters together point the same direction.

Here’s the beauty of these filters: they slow you down enough to hear the Shepherd in a world that monetizes noise. If what you sense fails any filter, hold. Don’t force it. Waiting on God is not wasting time; it’s making room for clarity. If it passes all four over time, don’t stall. Obey the light you have and trust God with the parts you don’t.

You may still ask, what if I miss it? Friend, you are not more powerful than God’s ability to lead you. He’s a Father, not a puzzle master. He can close the wrong doors and shepherd you back when you drift. Keep your Bible open, your heart soft, your community close, and your steps surrendered. Run every impression through Scripture alignment, Christlike fruit, wise counsel, and peace with conviction. Do this consistently, and you will learn the sound of His steps beside yours.
